Student assignment 1 · IR, RAG, agentic RAG

HotpotQA: From RAG to
Multi-Agent Investigation

An AI investigation assistant that answers questions from evidence spread over two documents, with a local Llama 3.1 8B on a laptop and no LLM training.

Research question: when does iterative or multi-agent investigation provide enough benefit over conventional RAG to justify the extra cost and complexity?
Short answer: rarely here. Better retrieval and a cheap rule-checked agent pay off; iteration and a multi-agent team add little over a bigger model in the same single agent.
1 · Problem

Multi-hop questions: the second document is never named

"In what year was the university where Sergei Aleksandrovich Tokarev was a professor founded?"
  • Hop 1 [Sergei Tokarev]: professor at Moscow State University
  • Hop 2 [Moscow State University]: "founded on January 25, 1755"
  • The question never mentions Moscow; the distractors are 7 other "Sergei Aleksandrovich"s

Why it's harder than ordinary QA

  • Evidence is split over two documents; neither answers alone
  • The second one is described only indirectly
  • Errors compound: miss either paragraph, or connect the wrong bridge entity
  • Distractors are adversarial: chosen to look like the question

Business use

Investigations, due diligence, support, research: answers must be right, traceable to evidence, cheap and fast.

2 · Dataset

HotpotQA (Yang et al., EMNLP 2018), distractor setting

2,000questions (seed 42) of 7,405 validation
19,260unique paragraphs: our closed corpus
  • 10 paragraphs per question: 2 gold + 8 TF-IDF distractors; supporting sentences annotated
  • 80% bridge, 20% comparison; answers short (83% ≤ 3 words)
  • Extractive: the answer is word-for-word in a gold paragraph for >99% of non-yes/no questions → retrieval is the bottleneck
  • Hop 2 is hidden: named in the question only 14% of the time (hop 1: 61%); a distractor overlaps the question more in 53%
  • But linked: the hop-2 title appears in the hop-1 text 66% of the time → follow links

Splits of the pool: test 500 (LLM runs: first 300) · train 1,000 (rankers) · tuning 500 (first 300 = "dev" for agent design).

Both@K = share of questions whose two gold paragraphs are both in the top K  ·  unsupported = the answer isn't found in the context it was given.

Protocol

  • Retrieval: 500 test questions, no LLM
  • End to end: 300 test questions; agents designed on 300 separate dev questions
  • Errors split into retrieval (a gold paragraph missing) vs reasoning failures
  • Per-question sign tests; 300 questions ≈ ±2.8 EM
  • Cost at hosted reference prices; latency re-timed in one clean session (cache off)

Backup · Q1–Q2

Why RAG? Why not a direct LLM?

  • The model doesn't know the facts but can read them: closed book 0.24 → RAG 0.37 on the same 100 questions; final system 0.537
  • Closed book invents confidently ("1880" for Moscow State University; correct 1755)
  • Verifiable: answers point to evidence; 97% of the checked agent's answers are found in what it read
  • Corpus can be updated or kept private without training (no LLM training allowed)

Why this retriever and Top-K?

  • Chunk = paragraph + title: short (gold median 62 words), one entity each
  • K = 5: both-gold 46% → 71% from K = 2 to 5, only +9 at K = 10 for 2× tokens; distractors cost ~6 EM
  • LambdaMART + Contriever-MSMARCO: best both@5 (0.924), hop 2 found 93%, no HotpotQA training in the dense model, 59 ms, trains in 1 s
  • Then top 5 sentences: same quality, 57% fewer tokens
Backup · Q3–Q4

When does BM25 beat dense retrieval?

  • BM25: exact term overlap × rarity (IDF), length-normalized; wins on rare exact strings (names, titles, numbers, codes, jargon), out of domain, with no training
  • Dense (bi-encoder): wins on paraphrase and indirect descriptions ("the university where X was a professor")
  • Here: BM25 0.544 beats unsupervised Contriever 0.470, loses to Contriever-MSMARCO 0.602 and BGE 0.706
  • Comparison questions name both entities → lexical/title signals do well
  • Best: hybrid; the reranker learns per question which to trust

Why rerank?

  • Two jobs: recall (cheap, wide pool: both gold in 99.2% of ~93) vs order (the LLM reads 5)
  • A reranker affords rich features on a small pool: 33 features; RRF 0.806 → LambdaMART 0.898 (48 / 2); both@2 0.47 → 0.71
  • Bi-encoder (separate encodings, fast, for search) vs cross-encoder (reads the pair jointly, accurate, one pass per pair, only for short lists)
  • Our LambdaMART can use cross-paragraph link features a pairwise cross-encoder can't see; 0.6 ms inference
Backup · Q5–Q6

Why did improved RAG still fail?

  • Bottleneck moved to the LLM: retrieval failures 24% → 5%, reasoning 20% → 29%
  • Even with gold paragraphs Llama 8B misses 42% (oracle 0.577)
  • Distractors: 0.50 with 5 paragraphs vs 0.56 with the 2 gold
  • Sentence selection drops evidence for 25% (EM 0.289 there vs 0.576)
  • Wrong hop / wrong type, comparisons and dates, format mismatches → motivated the Cycle 3 checks

What exactly makes it an agent?

  • The next retrieval depends on evidence observed at runtime: the decomposed agent's hop-2 query ("In what year was Moscow State University founded?") exists only after hop 1 is answered; 3.2 retrievals per question
  • The checked agent's route depends on runtime checks of its own draft: repair with different evidence and feedback (13%)
  • Not free-form ReAct: hops are planned up front and sufficiency is judged by code, deliberately (an 8B model judges its own evidence poorly); the cost: no early exit (Tokarev case)
Backup · Q7–Q8

Why multi-agent, not one agent with more tools?

  • Independence: one agent anchors on its own steps; two strategies fail on different questions (agree 70%, EM 0.72; disagree 30%; oracle pick 0.673)
  • Separated roles: investigators answer, the judge only verifies, with a clean context
  • Different models per role: the 14B judge only on the 30% of disagreements
  • Honest result: beats every Llama-only system (16 / 3) but ties one agent with the bigger model (26 / 24)

How do you stop loops and debug decisions?

  • Bounded by construction (LangGraph): ≤ 3 hops, ≤ 1 repair, exactly 2 judge calls; hard caps 2 / 6 / 10 LLM calls (checked / decomposed / team)
  • Stopping and routing decided by code (checks, consensus, order-swap rule), not the LLM
  • Every decision logged per question: draft, failed checks, trim rules, repair output, sub-questions and hop answers, judge picks in both orders
  • Temperature 0 + LLM cache → exact replay; one run yields the ablation
Backup · Q9

When would you deploy normal RAG instead?

  • Single-hop questions, or evidence in one document: one retrieval finds it; an agent only adds latency
  • When retrieval already brings both documents: comparison questions (both@5 99% already in Cycle 1)
  • Strict latency or cost budgets: Cycle 2c gives 0.503 at 1.5 s / 2.0 s and $0.014 per 1k with one call; iteration costs 4× latency for no EM gain, the team 6×
  • In practice the checked agent is "RAG plus rules": one call for 87% of questions and the same median latency, so it's a safe default; heavier agents only when hop 2 is often missed and accuracy is worth 4–6× the latency
